A 22 year old man presented to the emergency department with abdominal pain, jaundice, itching, and fever. His urine was dark and stools pale. Physical examination revealed temperature of 38.7°C, icterus, abdominal tenderness worst in the right upper quadrant with negative Murphy's sign, impalpable gall bladder, inflamed oropharynx with cervical and axillary lymphadenopathy. Laboratory tests showed liver function abnormalities with alkaline phosphatase 327, aspartate aminotransferase 358, and bilirubin 109 suggesting cholestasis. White cell count was elevated at 20.8, C reactive protein was raised at 17, and platelets were low at 168. Monospot test was positive for Epstein Barr virus, confirmed by positive Epstein Barr virus serology. Hepatitis A, B, and C serology were negative.
