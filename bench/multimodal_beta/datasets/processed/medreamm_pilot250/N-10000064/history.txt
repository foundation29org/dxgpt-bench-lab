A 39-year-old man presented to the emergency department during the summer with a 4-day history of fever with shaking chills, diffuse headache, and fatigue following his return from a 2-week trip to East Africa. During his trip to Tanzania, he had been on safari without animal contact but received mosquito bites, especially during the first days. He consumed cooked shrimp and lobster and a small amount of raw tuna. On the second-to-last day of the trip, he developed nausea, vomiting, and nonbloody diarrhea. Upon returning to Massachusetts, he experienced fever (38.3°C to 39.5°C), shaking chills, diffuse headache, and lower back pain. He reported weakness, anorexia, decreased urine output with dark urine, and blurry vision during fever episodes. His medical history included degenerative knee disease with previous glucocorticoid injections. On examination, he had a temperature of 38.9°C, blood pressure 113/62 mm Hg, facial flushing, mild scleral icterus, and dark brown urine.
