A 50-year-old postmenopausal woman (gravida 1, para 1) presented with abdominal distention, anorexia, and exertional dyspnoea. She had been in good health and was postmenopausal for 1 year. Physical examination revealed a markedly distended abdomen. CT of the abdomen and pelvis showed gross ascites extending under the diaphragm and a heterogeneous mass in the left adnexa, with part of the solid component having strong enhancement, plus small calcifications along the cystic wall. No lymphadenopathy or peritoneal dissemination was observed. Pelvic MRI showed the left pelvic mass with homogeneous low intensity on T1-weighted images and the solid parts including thick septa were uniformly low intensity on T2-weighted images and well enhanced on contrast-enhanced MRI, with no fatty components identified. Laboratory results showed serum CA 125 level of 1237 U/ml (normal <35 U/ml), while serum CEA, CA 19-9, and SCC levels were within normal ranges. Abdominal paracentesis performed multiple times showed benign mesothelial cells without malignant cells on cytological examination. ¹³¹I scintigraphy demonstrated strong uptake in the normal thyroid. The patient had no symptoms or clinical signs of thyroid hormone imbalance, with free-T3, free-T4, and thyroid stimulating hormone levels within normal ranges.
