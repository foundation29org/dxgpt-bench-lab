A 65-year-old male retired farmer presented with an inability to fully straighten the ring finger and little finger of his right hand, which had progressed over the past two years. He had recently noticed a 'rope like' swelling in his palm with no history of trauma. His medical history was unremarkable except for smoking cessation 10 years earlier with a 90 pack-year history. His father had a similar deformity. On examination, he had palpable cords to his middle, ring, and little fingers with palpable nodules on the volar aspect of all proximal phalanges, causing flexion contractures of the proximal interphalangeal joints. The cord and nodules in the ring finger were adherent to the overlying skin.
