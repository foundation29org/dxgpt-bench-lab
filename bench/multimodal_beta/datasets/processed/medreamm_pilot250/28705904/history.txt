A 20-year-old woman with a history of asthma presented to the emergency department with shortness of breath and sharp pains in her chest and neck. The pain worsened on deep inspiration. She had a cough producing small amounts of whitish sputum. She had experienced several previous asthma exacerbations and had not been using her inhalers regularly. On examination, her airway was patent with a generalized moderate wheeze in her chest, but no other obvious clinical signs. She could speak in full sentences and had normal oxygen saturations and temperature. She was mildly tachycardic and appeared slightly anxious. Blood tests showed a mild neutrophilia and elevated C reactive protein.
