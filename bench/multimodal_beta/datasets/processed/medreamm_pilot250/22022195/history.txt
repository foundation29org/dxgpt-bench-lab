A 29-year-old man presented to the hospital after several weeks of lumbar pain and fever up to 38°C. He had no significant past medical history. Physical examination revealed vital signs of blood pressure 130/80 mmHg, heart rate 121/min, and body temperature 38.2°C. There was no lymphadenopathy or hepatosplenomegaly detected on examination. Laboratory tests showed anemia with hematocrit 31.8% and hemoglobin 11.8 g/dL, white blood cell count 3.92 × 10³/μL, elevated liver enzymes with AST 78 IU/L and ALT 71 IU/L, and markedly elevated lactate dehydrogenase 1,591 IU/L. Serum cortisol level was 17.47 μg/dL at 8 AM and ACTH level was 12.1 pg/mL (normal range: 10-90). Ten days after admission, the patient developed pancytopenia with WBC 2.88 × 10³/μL, Hb 10.7 g/dL, and platelets 129 × 10³/μL. Bone marrow biopsy was negative for specific hematologic or malignant disorder.
