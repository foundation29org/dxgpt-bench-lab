A 6-month-old boy presented to the emergency department with gastrointestinal bleeding and abdominal pain. Two days prior, he began having intermittent episodes of abdominal pain that woke him from sleep, during which he would cry and pull his legs toward his chest. His abdomen felt rigid during these episodes. He had a history of infantile colic and gastroesophageal reflux previously treated with ranitidine. He was on low-lactose cow milk-based formula and had recently started pureed foods. The patient had three bowel movements with increasing amounts of reddish discoloration, culminating in a bowel movement that appeared to be almost entirely blood with a large clot. Initial vital signs showed temperature 36.5°C, pulse 178/min, blood pressure 95/52 mmHg, respiratory rate 24/min, and oxygen saturation 100%. Physical examination revealed diffuse abdominal tenderness, greater on the right side, with no masses or external anal fissures. Later examination showed pallor, with pulse 168/min and blood pressure 94/36 mmHg.
