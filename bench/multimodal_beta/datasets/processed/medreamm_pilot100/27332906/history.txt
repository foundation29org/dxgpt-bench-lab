A 57-year-old man presented to the emergency department with a 12-hour history of hematuria. Several hours after intravenous urography was performed with iodinated contrast material, generalized pustular eruptions developed, with multiple coalescing vesicles and pus-filled bullae observed on the trunk, arms, and legs. Laboratory studies revealed neutrophilia and mild eosinophilia with 55% neutrophils and 8% eosinophils, and a white-cell count of 6500 per cubic millimeter. Histopathological examination of the lesions showed pseudoepitheliomatous hyperplasia, full-thickness epidermal necrosis, and a diffuse neutrophilic dermal infiltrate. Microbiologic studies were negative.
