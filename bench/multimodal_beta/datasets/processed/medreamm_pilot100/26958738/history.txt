An 81-year-old man presented to the ICU 10 days after a 4-week cruise in New Caledonia, Vanuatu, the Solomon Islands, and New Zealand. He was reported to have been in perfect health during the cruise. On examination, he was febrile (39.1°C) and comatose with a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 6. He exhibited hemiplegia of the left side, paresis of the right upper limb, normal tendon reflexes, and a Babinski sign on the left side. A transient rash was observed within 48 hours of admission. Cerebrospinal fluid analysis showed leukocyte count of 41/mm³ (98% polymorphonuclear leukocytes), protein level of 76 mg/dL, and CSF:blood glucose ratio of 0.75.
